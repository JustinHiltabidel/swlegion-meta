**Body:**

Hi,

I'm Justin Hiltabidel, a competitive Star Wars: Legion player and data scientist. I'm building an analytics tool for mid-tier competitive players — the folks trying to break into the top of the RTT / regional scene — and I wanted to reach out before doing anything technical with your platform.

Longshanks is obviously the foundation of the competitive Legion data ecosystem. I don't want to build anything that competes with it or operates around your terms. I'd rather have a conversation up front.

**What I'm building, in short:**

A tiered product built around personal analytics for the games Longshanks *doesn't* track — casual shop games, practice sessions, unrated leagues, playtest games while tuning a list. My core premise is that these games make up 80–90% of a competitive player's total play, and today no one tracks them. Players submit their own games (list URLs from Tabletop Admiral or Legion List Builder, plus scenario and result) and get personal statistics back: matchup win rates, objective breakdowns, and — the part I'm most excited about — trend analysis over time, so a player can see "my win rate improved from 45% to 58% since I swapped Grievous for Dooku." That kind of personal, longitudinal analytics doesn't exist anywhere in the Legion ecosystem right now.

Above that, a free newsletter covers meta commentary and tournament recaps (using publicly discussed data, not your private data), and a paid tier adds list scoring and matchup planning. The premium tier is where the personal analytics live.

**Why I'm reaching out:**

The natural gap in this design is a player's rated tournament games. Those *do* live on Longshanks. Ideally, a player using my tool wouldn't have to double-enter their tournament games — they'd log casual games with me, tournament games with you, and see everything in one place.

I can imagine a few ways that might work:

1. **Any form of read access** — API, data export, or sanctioned scraping guidelines — that would let a user pull their own event history into my tool
2. **A user-driven export path** — if you have or would consider a "download my data" feature, that would let players move their tournament data themselves without me touching your platform
3. **Something you'd propose** that fits your model better than what I've thought of

Whatever the answer, I want to do this the right way. If the answer is "no access, please don't scrape," that's a real answer and I'll design around it — leaning entirely on user-submitted casual/practice data, which is arguably the more differentiated positioning anyway.

**What I can offer in return:**

- Public credit on the site, in the newsletter, and in any published analysis
- Explicit non-competition — my content is decision-support and personal analytics; yours is the tournament infrastructure and community meta layer. Different products.
- Coordination on anything that touches your data — if you want to review how it's used or set limits, I'm happy to work within them
- Whatever else would be useful to you

Happy to jump on a call, exchange emails, or just get a quick "here's how we think about this" reply. No rush on my end — I'd rather move slowly and correctly than fast and wrong.

Thanks for building Longshanks. It's the reason the competitive Legion scene has a shared truth to argue about, and it's what makes a project like mine possible in the first place.

## Notes on the draft

**What this email does deliberately:**

- **Leads with humility, not confidence.** "Before doing anything technical with your platform" signals respect for their ownership immediately.
- **Names the personal-analytics angle explicitly** — trend analysis over time is the standout feature, called out with the specific "45% to 58% since Grievous → Dooku" example so they see it concretely, not abstractly.
- **Frames the product as complementary, not competitive.** The "games Longshanks doesn't track" positioning does the work here — makes clear you're not trying to be them, you're covering a gap they don't cover.
- **Offers three specific paths forward.** Easier for them to say yes to a menu than to invent a solution.
- **Gives them an easy out.** "If the answer is no, I'll design around it" removes the pressure — they're more likely to engage when they know there's no cost to declining.
- **Closes with genuine credit.** The "shared truth to argue about" line lands because it's true and specific to what Longshanks actually is.

**What to personalize before sending:**

- Your faction, your region, your relevant competitive context — establishes you're a player, not just a builder
- Email signature with real contact info
- Optional: reference a specific Longshanks event you attended recently if it's natural

**What not to change:**

- Length. It's long on purpose. Short outreach emails read as templated. This reads as considered.
- The three-option menu. Concrete asks convert.
- The "no is fine" clause. Removes pressure, increases response rate.
